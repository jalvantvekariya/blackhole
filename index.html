ABOUT THE SIMULATION

Scientific Concepts

What is a Black Hole?
A region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing, including light, can escape across its event horizon ($r_s = 2GM/c^2$).

What is an Accretion Disk?
A disk of superheated gas and plasma orbiting a massive central body. Gravitational shear forces convert potential energy into extreme radiation.

What is Gravitational Lensing?
General Relativity shows that mass warps spacetime. Photons traveling near the event horizon follow curved geodesics, causing light from the far side of the disk to bend over and under the horizon.

What is a Photon Ring?
A narrow glowing band composed of photons orbitally bound in unstable circular paths at $1.5\,r_s$ before escaping to observers or falling into the horizon.

Frame Dragging & Ergosphere
A rotating (Kerr) black hole drags spacetime itself along with its spin. The ergosphere is an oblate region outside the horizon where spacetime dragging forces all objects to rotate alongside the black hole.

Hawking Radiation
Quantum vacuum fluctuations near the event horizon generate virtual particle pairs, creating microscopic thermal emission. For astrophysical black holes ($M \ge 10\,M_\odot$), Hawking radiation is astronomically weak ($T \sim 10^{-8}\,\text{K}$) and negligible compared to accretion glow.

Scientific Disclaimer:
This simulation is an educational visualization inspired by General Relativity and simplified accretion models. Physical parameters, Doppler shifts, and field geometries are relativistic approximations optimized for real-time WebGL rendering rather than full numerical relativity solutions.
